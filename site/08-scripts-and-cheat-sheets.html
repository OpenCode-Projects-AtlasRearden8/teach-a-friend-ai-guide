Chapter 8 — Scripts and Cheat Sheets

Phrasings to carry, phrasings to skip, and printable one-pagers. Steal every word; these have been tested on real friends, kitchens, and couches.

8.1 Say this, not that

Instead of… Say… Why
"It's really easy" "It takes everyone a couple of tries" Removes the "am I slow?" trap
"No, that's wrong" "Actually, that works too — here's a shorter way" Protects the instinct
"All you do is type" "Let's just say what you'd tell a person" Gives a metaphor, not a shrug
"See? Not so hard!" "You did that whole thing yourself" Credits agency, not alchemy
"You should get ChatGPT Plus" "The free one is plenty for what we're doing today" No shopping in session one
"Ask it anything!" (blank box looming) "Want one of these three, or say your own?" Kills the freeze
"Let me do it real quick" "Want to take a run at it, or should we do it together?" Keeps their hands on keys
"It just hallucinates sometimes" "Sometimes it makes stuff up and says it like it's sure — always worth a check on anything that matters" Plain language, real warning
"You typed your whole question like a search — don't do that" "Full sentences actually work better — it's more like texting a person than googling" Confirms instinct
"I'll set up the account for you" "This part's yours — I'll look away while you pick a password" Privacy = dignity

8.2 Openers for session one

Pick the one matching your person's vibe:

  • Warm-casual: "One hour, one real thing — if it's not for you, that's a fine ending too. Deal?"
  • Skeptic: "It's a pattern-finisher, not a mind. Bring your sharpest 'yeah, right' — we'll test it on your stuff."
  • Nervous: "Nothing here can break. Worst case, we ask it again."
  • Busy: "Forty minutes, one thing, done properly. I brought coffee."

8.3 Rescue lines for the hard moments

  • Blank-box freeze: "Big blank boxes fluster everyone. Want one of these three…"
  • Disappointment at a bad answer: "Okay — what would you change about that? …tell it that."
  • "I broke it": "You can't break it. It's just words — worst case, we start a fresh chat."
  • "This is a dumb question": "Nope — that's actually a great question; here's why smart people ask it…"
  • Awkward silence (theirs): wait five full seconds. Then: "want to try, or want me to do half?"
  • Tech failure: "Even my setup does this. Watch — I'll show you how I unstuck it last time."
  • "Am I too old / slow for this?": "The typing is the least important part. The knowing-what-you-want part — you're ahead of most people on that."

8.4 Celebration lines (Chapter 6, pocket version)

  • "You did the whole thing — I typed nothing."
  • "You just told it to change its mind. Power move."
  • "That card smells like you. Your sister's gonna hear your voice."
  • "You caught its mistake yourself. That's the actual skill."
  • "Resilience points for sticking through the weird answers."

8.5 The one-pager: sticky note for their fridge

Print or copy. Six lines, their handwriting is fine:

MY AI CHEAT SHEET
 1. Ask like it's a person. Full sentences beat keywords.
 2. Not happy? TELL it what to change ("shorter, friendlier").
 3. Ask follow-ups. It's a conversation, not a vending machine.
 4. Never type: addresses, ID/insurance numbers, passwords, other
    people's private stuff.
 5. Big-stakes stuff (money/health/legal)? Double-check with a pro.
 6. Can't break it. Worst case: new chat, try again.

8.6 The one-pager: helper's session card (yours, keep in pocket)

BEFORE: task picked? backup task? machine charged? openers decided?
MIN 0-5:   low bar sentence, them in the seat, coffee, phone face down.
MIN 5-10:  one real live demo (3 min), then "your turn — you type."
MIN 10-40: first real task. Their hands. Two-minute-stuck rule.
           Wobble menu: hmm → maybe me → try again → park it.
MIN 40-50: what-if game, ONE solo side quest, fear question.
MIN 50-60: write the one thing (their hand), celebrate specifically,
           next touch booked, STOP ON TIME.
AFTER:     one warm text naming their move. Log: clicked/stuck/next.

8.7 The privacy poem (memorize the four lines)

Give them this, verbally, once, in any session — it sticks better than rules:

Addresses and numbers, never share. Passwords live in your head alone. Fun things, surely; big things, beware. When in doubt, what's typed goes to a phone.

(Okay, it's not Shakespeare — but four mnemonics survive the week, which is exactly the point.)

8.9 Sitting-down-to-start scripts for tricky follow-ups

  • "Can you do my taxes?" — "It can explain the forms and help you keep the papers organized — the actual filing and the numbers should go through the official way, or a pro. Let's use it for the annoying organizing part."
  • "Is my husband cheating?" (any relationship-medical-legal reading of the tea leaves) — "That's a question for a person, not a word-machine. It can't read people; it can only write about people. Let's not ask it that one."
  • "Will it replace my job?" — honest, kind, brief: "Some parts of jobs are getting easier and some parts people keep. I don't know your future and neither does it — but knowing how to use it is never a losing position. Want to look at what it's good for in your field?"
  • "I don't want the computer reading my stuff" — "Completely fair, and a good instinct. We can use it for the least-personal stuff only — recipes, letters, gift ideas — and keep everything private off it. That's a totally coherent way to live with it."

Answer policies, not persuasions. People keep the dignity of their own conclusions.

Chapter 8 in one breath

Steal the lines, print the one-pagers, memorize the poem and the pocket celebrations, and answer the hard questions with policies and warmth — never with a verdict on someone's life.

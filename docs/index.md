# Teach a Friend AI: A Warm Manual for Being the Helper

*A manual from Asdale Dynamics, LLC — one friend, one hour at a time.*

## Who this is for

You already use AI. Maybe you write with it, plan with it, or just chat with it.
And someone in your life has noticed. Your mom asked "what's that thing you're
always typing to?" Your husband rolled his eyes at it. Your roommate keeps
saying "I should learn that someday."

This manual is for **you** — the person holding the door open. Not a class,
not a curriculum, not tech support. One friend, one hour at a time, showing
another human being how AI can make *their* life a little easier.

## What this manual is (and isn't)

**It is:**

- A guide to running kind, low-pressure one-on-one sessions
- A pacing map for different kinds of learners (seniors, skeptical spouses,
  students, busy friends)
- A script for choosing the *first real task* — the one that makes it click
- Templates for celebrating wins without being patronizing
- A plan for handing off control safely, so you're not their helper forever

**It is not:**

- A course. There are no quizzes and nobody "fails."
- Household tech support. If you're fixing a printer or untangling a Wi-Fi
  password, that's a different job. This is *teaching,* not *rescuing.*
- A lecture. The friend does the typing. Always.

## The one rule everything hangs on

> **Never make the person feel dumb.**
> Not in your words, not in your pauses, not in how fast you reach for the
> keyboard.

Everything in this manual — the pacing, the phrasing, the win celebrations,
the handoff — is built to keep that promise. If you remember only one page of
this book, it's that sentence.

## The shape of a full helping run

For most learners, the whole journey is **four to six one-hour sessions spread
over a month or two.** That's it. The arc:

1. **Session one:** the first real task, done together, they type.
2. **Weeks between:** they practice alone once; you check in once, warmly.
3. **Session two:** a slightly bigger task, more of *their* typing.
4. **Sessions three+:** you coach more, do less; introduce saving, privacy,
   and "what to do when AI is wrong."
5. **Handoff:** they own it. Your new role is friend, not helper.

Most of the anxiety around "teaching someone AI" comes from imagining a
classroom. There is no classroom. There's a couch, a laptop, one task, and an
hour. That's the whole product.

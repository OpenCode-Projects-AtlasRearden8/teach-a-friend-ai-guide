# Chapter 6 — Celebrate and Continue

Celebration is not the dessert of teaching. It's the engine. This chapter is
about marking real progress without a single drop of condescension — then
keeping momentum between sessions, gently, for a month or two.

## 6.1 Why celebration matters (mechanically)

Beginners run on evidence. Learning-in-public is a séance: they want proof the
seance works. Every win you name is a piece of evidence that they *can do
this thing,* and un-named wins evaporate by Tuesday. The helping run
continues or dies based on whether wins get *witnessed.*

## 6.2 The two kinds of wins

Track both, celebrate both:

1. **Output wins** — something got made: a card sent, a plan printed, an
   email finally answered.
2. **Move wins** — some*thing they *did* got better: they asked a follow-up
   question unprompted, they told the AI to be shorter, they rejected a bad
   answer on their own judgment, they retyped the prompt for a second try.

Move wins are the gold. They're also invisible to the learner the first
several times. Naming a move win sounds like:

> "Did you notice what you did there? It gave you a stiff answer and you
> just — bossed it around. 'Shorter, friendlier.' That's exactly what
> experienced users do. That wasn't luck."

## 6.3 How to celebrate without being patronizing

The line between *witnessing* and *condescending* is thin. Hold it with
these four moves:

| Condescending (avoid) | Warm and true (use) |
|---|---|
| "Great job!! 🎉" | "That card reads like *you* — your sister will hear your voice in it." |
| "You're so smart for a…" anything | "That's a genuinely better prompt than most people's first week." |
| "See? It's not so hard!" | "You did that whole thing yourself. I typed nothing." |
| Over-cheer at trivial things | Specific, honest, one sentence, then move on. |

Tone rules:

- **Specific > superlative.** "You kept asking until it gave you what you
  wanted" beats "awesome."
- **Credit their agency, not the tool.** "You wrote that question well" —
  never "it's so smart, right?"
- **Credit process, especially on bad days.** If the session was rough,
  find the *one* real move: "You stayed with it when the thing kept
  misunderstanding you. That's resilience most users never develop."
- **Match their celebration style.** Some people want a fist bump; some
  want a quiet nod; some want you to *tell their spouse.* Follow their
  lead, exactly.

## 6.4 The wins journal

Give them (or make together) a small **wins journal** — paper first. Three
lines minimum per entry:

```
WIN: ___________ (one line, plain language)
HOW:  ___________ (what they did — the move)
NEXT: ___________ (optional: thing to try sometime)
```

On paper, in folder or fridge-door position. Old-school works because it's
*tangible*, reviewable without a screen, and — crucially — *theirs.* If they
prefer digital, a notes-app list is fine; the point is a place wins live
where bad days can't delete them.

Reread it at the start of every session (Chapter 1 §1.6). Begin each session
by reading their last two wins *back to them.* Almost nobody does this. It
takes forty seconds and it's the cheapest morale machine ever invented.

## 6.5 Between sessions: the gentle rhythm

One touch between sessions. Only one. Choose by learner (Chapter 4):

- **Senior:** a short warm text mid-week; attach the one thing *they made*,
  if it exists ("that trip list is printed and in my bag"). Ask a light
  question at the end; welcome the phone call if they call instead.
- **Skeptic:** send nothing. Let them come to you. If they do, be matter-
  of-fact; curiosity, not victory laps.
- **Student:** share one article or meme if relevant, zero lectures.
- **Busy friend:** nothing until they ping. Respect the velocity.
- **Dabbler:** one genuinely interesting tidbit tied to their flow, not a
  newsletter of four.

If a "between touch" goes unanswered: **let it breathe.** Silence between
sessions is breaching no contract. The next session still happens on time.

## 6.6 Sessions two through six: the spiral

One-hour, once-a-week-or-two rhythm; each session is the *same shape*
(Chapter 5) with the guided edge slowly removed:

- **Session two:** A slightly bigger version of their first task; 70% their
  typing. Introduce the wins journal and reread past entries together.
- **Session three:** They arrive with a task *they picked.* Your job: ask
  questions, narrate less, coach more. Introduce saving & organizing (where
  their work actually lives).
- **Session four:** The judgment hour — "what to do when the AI is wrong,"
  privacy hygiene, which jobs to hand it and which never to. Mostly
  conversation over their real current task.
- **Session five:** They run the hour; you're the Spotify in the passenger
  seat: minimal prompting, question-answering only. End of the session,
  preview the handoff (Chapter 7).
- **Session six (if needed):** The handoff session proper. Make it
  ceremonial-light: mostly a normal session, ending with the handoff
  conversation.

**The decreasing-intervention rule:** every session, you do visibly less.
If your hands touched the keyboard more in session four than session two,
something's off — recalibrate the next hour.

## 6.7 When weeks get skipped

Life happens — holidays, unpleasant medical weeks, a week when your friend's
world catches fire. Handle it like this:

1. **No guilt-tripping in any direction.** Yours, theirs, none.
2. **Resume, don't restart.** "Last time we did cards — want to warm up
   with one of those, or something new?" Rust is normal; the journal
   rebuilds confidence in four minutes.
3. **Shorter is better than postponed.** A twenty-minute win restores the
   rhythm; "next month" dissolves it.

## 6.8 The five-week rule (a caution for you)

Some helping runs end because life interferes. Many end because the helper
*never let go* — week ten arrives and the helper is still answering
logistics texts at midnight, secretly pleased, secretly exhausted. Watch
for it in yourself (Chapter 7 diagnoses the drift). The goal is a friend who
*occasionally asks you things now and then* — not a client subscription you
forgot to cancel.

## Chapter 6 in one breath

Witness the output wins *and* the move wins — specifically, briefly, agency-
credited. Keep a wins journal, touch base once between sessions in their
style, spiral the sessions gently outward, and drop your intervention every
week like sand through fingers.
